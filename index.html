Gotcha!

You just scanned a QR code from a stranger's slide — without checking where it goes.

That's exactly how "Quishing" works

Quishing = QR-code phishing. A malicious QR can silently open a fake login page, start a payment, or push you to install malware — and you'd never see the real address first.

Right now this was harmless — it's a live demo in your cyber-security session. But in the wild, that one scan is all an attacker needs.

The rule: treat an unknown QR exactly like an unknown link — pause, check where it really goes, and don't trust it just because it's printed on something official-looking.

Red flags for a bad QR

›A QR sticker pasted over another one (parking meters, posters, menus).
›It asks you to log in, pay, or enter an OTP after scanning.
›The preview URL is a shortener or a look-alike domain.
›It arrived unexpectedly — in an email, a "KYC" SMS, or a random flyer.
